Section A: Filtration & Separation Techniques

Questions 26-33

26 Analysis

A forensic scientist has a mixture of sand, salt, and water from a crime scene. Which separation technique should be used FIRST, and why?

27 Application

After filtering a mixture of chalk powder and water, a student notices that the filtrate is still slightly cloudy. What is the MOST likely explanation?

28 Analysis

A scientist filters a mixture and finds that BOTH the residue and filtrate are needed for further analysis. What must they ensure during the filtration process?

29 True/False

If you filter a sugar solution, the sugar will remain in the residue because it is a solid.

30 Application

A student sets up filtration equipment incorrectly and the filter paper tears during filtration. What would be the consequence?

31 Analysis

You have a mixture of sand, salt, and iron filings. You need to separate all three components. Which sequence of separation techniques would be MOST effective?

32 True/False

The reason filtration cannot separate dissolved substances is because dissolved particles are at the molecular level and are too small to be trapped by filter paper.

33 Application

In water treatment plants, filtration is used to remove solid particles from drinking water. Why is this an important step BEFORE the water reaches homes?

Section B: Types of Mixtures

Questions 34-42

34 Analysis

A chemist has three beakers: Beaker A contains clear saltwater, Beaker B contains muddy water where particles settle, and Beaker C contains milk which is cloudy but particles don't settle. Classify each mixture correctly.

35 Application

A student makes orange juice by squeezing fresh oranges. After a few minutes, they notice pulp settling at the bottom. What type of mixture is this fresh orange juice, and what does the settling indicate?

36 Analysis

Why is smoke considered a colloid rather than a suspension, even though both appear cloudy?

37 True/False

All cloudy mixtures are suspensions.

38 Application

A student wants to determine if a clear liquid in a beaker is pure water or a salt solution. Which method would work BEST?

39 Analysis

Air is a mixture of nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, and other gases. Why is air considered a mixture rather than a pure substance?

40 Application

A food scientist needs to create a product with a long shelf life. They observe that suspensions tend to separate over time while solutions remain mixed. What does this suggest about which type of mixture would be better for their product?

41 True/False

Sea water is an impure substance because it contains dissolved salts and minerals, not just pure H₂O molecules.

42 Analysis

A chemist has a mixture where light can pass through but the path of the light beam becomes visible (known as the Tyndall effect). What does this observation tell us about the mixture?

Section C: Solutions & Solubility

Questions 43-50

43 Application

A student adds sugar to cold water and stirs. Some sugar dissolves but some remains at the bottom even after stirring for several minutes. The student then heats the water and all the sugar dissolves. What can be concluded?

44 Analysis

When making a salt solution, a student notices that after adding 36g of salt to 100ml of water at room temperature, no more salt will dissolve. What has occurred?

45 Application

A factory needs to separate salt from seawater to produce table salt. They heat large shallow pools of seawater in the sun. What is the scientific principle behind this method?

46 True/False

Oil is soluble in water, which is why oil spills spread across the ocean surface.

47 Analysis

Two students each make a copper sulfate solution using 100ml of water. Student A uses cold water and can only dissolve 20g of copper sulfate before it becomes saturated. Student B uses hot water and dissolves 40g before saturation. What does this demonstrate?

48 Application

During an evaporation experiment, a student heats a salt solution too strongly and notices some salt "spitting" out of the dish and the solution boiling violently. What would be a better approach?

49 Analysis

A hot, saturated solution of potassium nitrate is left to cool slowly. Crystals begin to form as it cools. Why does this happen?

50 Application

A pharmaceutical company needs to purify a medicine by removing impurities. They dissolve the medicine in a minimum amount of hot solvent, then allow it to cool slowly. Pure medicine crystals form while impurities remain dissolved. What separation technique is this?
